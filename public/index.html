Why now

We stopped showing up for each other.

The goals didn’t go anywhere: the first big climb, the race, the thing you want to build. The people we used to do them with did.

Willpower is overrated. Spotters aren’t. You don’t need more discipline. You need people who notice when you don’t show up.

Our line, inspired by Modern Wisdom #1158, Chris Williamson’s conversation with psychologist Angela Duckworth (Oct 2026), which covers why groups make goals easier and why modern life is making us more isolated.5

FAQ

Fair questions.

What is Goal Groups?

A place to post a goal, gather spotters who see you show up, and build a reputation for following through. Crews are there when you want company.

What does a spotter do?

A spotter follows your check-ins and cheers you on. Crew does it with you. Spotters keep an eye on you. It’s a light way to back someone, and it tends to keep the spotter going too.

How does reputation work?

It’s built from three things: spotted show-ups, your show-up rate, and goals finished. Those roll up into a tier from C to S. You can’t buy it; you can only show up for it.

What does “locks when it fills” mean?

If you open a crew, it starts once enough people join, like 6 of 8. If it doesn’t fill by its deadline, it rolls into a new round. Solo goals with spotters start whenever you say.

Is it safe to post goals publicly?

Pins only show a first name and a town, never an address. Spotters see check-ins, not your location. First meetups for crews are meant for public places, and you can stay remote.

Is this only in Colorado?

We’re starting in Colorado, beginning with Denver, but plenty of goals work remotely, like writing, building and habits. Post one from anywhere.
